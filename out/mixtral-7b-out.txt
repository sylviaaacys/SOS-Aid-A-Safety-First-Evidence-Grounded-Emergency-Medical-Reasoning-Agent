CPR, or cardiopulmonary resuscitation, is a lifesaving technique that is used to restore blood circulation and breathing in a person who has stopped breathing or whose heart has stopped beating. It is typically performed by laypeople who have been trained in the technique, but it can also be performed by healthcare professionals.

To perform CPR, you should follow these steps:

1. Call 911 or your local emergency number immediately. If you are alone with the person, call for help before beginning CPR.
2. Position the person on their back on a firm, flat surface.
3. Open the person's airway by tilting their head back and lifting their chin.
4. Check for breathing by listening for breath sounds and feeling for air on your cheek.
5. If the person is not breathing, start chest compressions. Place the heel of one hand on the person's chest, on the lower half of the breastbone, and place your other hand on top of the first hand. Keep your arms straight and press down on the chest with the heel of your hand to a depth of about 2 inches. Release the pressure and repeat.
6. After 30 compressions, give two breaths. Pinch the person's nose shut and give two breaths, each lasting about one second. The breaths should make the person's chest rise.
7. Continue with this cycle of 30 compressions and two breaths until emergency medical personnel arrive.
